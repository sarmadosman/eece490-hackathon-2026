# Validation plans for the three business patterns

These are retained item/branch validation plans. The current primary [customer-continuity plans](pilot.md) follow the user's request for less common customer-level patterns.

The project identifies two or three profit-oriented decisions from pre/post comparisons. Each proposed action needs its own validation. Food offers are one possible test, not the required project outcome. No real pilot or cost audit has been conducted.

## 1. Product-specific promotion test

At Harbour, investigate Iced Latte and Frozen Yoghurt Cup with separate product-level economics. Document current eligibility, discount depth, list prices, and availability. Randomize normal service/current promotion versus an agreed alternative depth or eligibility rule within contemporaneous date/daypart blocks. Assignment and eligibility must be recorded before the purchase decision; historical discounted purchases are not a randomized control group.

Use total gross contribution per assigned eligible customer/order as the primary measure, including nonbuyers under a denominator defined before assignment. Include changes in other basket items and incremental operating costs. Record units, realized prices, substitution, availability, waste, and service effects. Set an economically meaningful effect, variance-based sample size, and fixed analysis horizon before launch. If using randomized time blocks, analyze at the block level; account for repeated customers where possible. Ensure arms have comparable Ramadan/Lent and promotion exposure. Do not conclude from short-term units alone.

The reported 7.6%/7.0% volume-loss tolerances are accounting thresholds, not acceptance or elasticity estimates. A practical test must determine actual response and broader basket effects.

## 2. Branch cost-definition audit and limited change

For Garden, verify the same-product cost basis against Harbour: ingredients, quantities, recipes, supplier invoices, delivery, portion/quality, allocation rules, and data configuration. Start with material cost differences, not all products simultaneously. Correct a confirmed recording issue or test an actually feasible procurement/recipe change while tracking quality, availability, price, and demand.

Compare net operating contribution after implementation costs, with an appropriate concurrent comparison where feasible. Keep quantities and quality explicit. A recorded peer gap is neither an achievable saving nor proof of inefficiency. The 10%/25%/50% gap-closure scenarios are assumptions to challenge, not targets established by the dataset.

## 3. Preparation/product trial

For a small set of products such as Harbour Quinoa Bowl, compare a current preparation rule with a recent matching-weekday rule using chronological historical validation first. Then run a limited operational trial with preparation, unsold units, stockouts, available hours, incremental labor, and substitution recorded. Do not treat observed sales as uncensored demand or a reduction in forecast error as realized waste savings.

If testing whether suggestions create additional purchases, randomize usual service versus product suggestions among eligible customers before the suggestion. Food-pair economics and the [optional bakery-versus-meal pilot](food_offer_pilot.md) are supporting designs. Do not label products appropriate for Lent/fasting customers without ingredient and customer-requirement validation.

## Decision rule

For each action, management should pre-agree the minimum worthwhile contribution improvement and service/quality/waste limits. Expand only when the measured operating benefit, uncertainty, and guardrails justify it. A result observed in one calendar phase or branch may not generalize to another. Report measured outcomes separately from conditional scenario values.
