# Customer-continuity validation plans

These are proposed tests, not campaigns already run. No customer was contacted. Preserve current policy as a randomized comparison and record assignment before the intervention. Customer IDs must be valid and globally consistent across branches; operational use also requires the business's normal customer-contact permissions and process.

## 1. Distinguish breadth from repeat-customer behavior

Freeze membership using an independent earlier observation period; record calibration dates and repeat-day threshold. Do not define the cohort from the period whose frequency change will then be reported. Include every assigned account, including zero-visit members. Track identified-account capture and use a common fixed follow-up interval.

Choose a specific service or repeat-visit friction hypothesis, such as account recognition or availability information. Randomize eligible accounts between current service and the chosen intervention. Measure chain-wide recorded contribution per assigned account after intervention costs, visit-days, orders, and customer capture. Report previously observed reactivations separately from first-observed accounts; do not call either verified acquisition without outside history.

## 2. Test chain-wide continuity before branch-loss recovery

Create branch-customer cohorts at a fixed date and check chain-wide activity before forming an absence-based service/recovery list. Accounts observed only at other branches remain active chain accounts. Test a branch-independent continuity/routing process against current practice at the customer level; keep one assignment across branches and avoid counting transferred revenue as new chain revenue.

Record service delivery, eligible accounts, contacts, costs, branch use, account recognition, and chain-wide contribution. If current branch-loss outreach does not exist, the avoided-contact-cost scenario is inapplicable. Do not sum overlapping branch cohorts or claim war-driven relocation from these observations.

## 3. Compare short-horizon versus staged inactivity policies

Among accounts eligible under an agreed short-absence rule, randomize current timing versus a specified staged alternative. Use the same complete 56-day outcome horizon for both arms. Keep assigned accounts with no observed return in the denominator; do not score accounts without sufficient follow-up as failures. Measure contribution, intervention costs, timing of observed return, and service outcomes. If repeat assignments are possible, maintain the original customer assignment or account for dependence.

Do not assume 56 days is optimal or that all delayed returns would occur without intervention. Historical late returns might already be influenced by promotions or customer service. A control group and pre-agreed sample size/horizon are needed to estimate incremental value.

## Shared design requirements

Block assignment by calendar period and relevant baseline behavior so arms have comparable Ramadan/Lent and event exposure. Pre-agree a worthwhile dollar improvement, sample size based on variance and clustering, fixed analysis horizon, and service guardrails. Track missing-ID/capture changes and report effects only in the population measured. Evaluate one clear policy per test, rather than choosing a favorable subgroup afterward.
