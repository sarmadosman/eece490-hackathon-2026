# Validation plans for the three-part playbook

These are proposed tests, not campaigns already run. No customer was contacted. Preserve current policy as a randomized comparison and record assignment before the intervention. Customer IDs must be valid and globally consistent across branches; operational use also requires the business's normal customer-contact permissions and process.

## 1. Demand and preparation

Test the recent four-matching-weekdays preparation rule against the existing rule at a branch or in randomized operating blocks where feasible. Record quantities prepared, unsold units, stockout periods, availability, wait times, sales, and incremental operating costs. Compare operating contribution and pre-agreed waste/service criteria. Lower historical forecast error is supporting evidence, not measured waste savings or proof of no material demand change.

## 2. Promotion holdout

Randomize eligible operating days, time blocks, or branches into the agreed promotion and a contemporaneous holdout. Preserve comparable calendar exposure, record assignment before implementation, and analyze at the randomization unit with appropriate dependence and spillover handling. Record promoted and non-promoted items, total branch revenue and recorded contribution, discounts, availability, waste, substitution, later purchases where feasible, and incremental operating costs. Pre-agree the worthwhile dollar effect, sample size, and fixed follow-up before the test.

Expand only if the randomized comparison shows a pre-agreed worthwhile improvement in total branch gross contribution after discounts and incremental operating costs. Report promoted-category units and the approximately 29% same-mix break-even benchmark as supporting measures.

The historical $471.55 is discount relative to listed prices, not a measured causal loss. The 60 overlapping non-promotion comparisons describe background variation; they are not independent experiments, a calibrated confidence interval, or a power analysis.

## 3. Customer continuity and timing

### Customer cohorts and repeat behavior

Freeze membership using an independent earlier observation period; record calibration dates and repeat-day threshold. Do not define the cohort from the period whose frequency change will then be reported. Include every assigned account, including zero-visit members. Track identified-account capture and use a common fixed follow-up interval.

Choose a specific service or repeat-visit friction hypothesis, such as account recognition or availability information. Randomize eligible accounts between current service and the chosen intervention. Measure chain-wide recorded contribution per assigned account after intervention costs, visit-days, orders, and customer capture. Report previously observed reactivations separately from first-observed accounts; do not call either verified acquisition without outside history.

### Chain-wide continuity before branch-loss recovery

Create branch-customer cohorts at a fixed date and check chain-wide activity before forming an absence-based service/recovery list. Accounts observed only at other branches remain active chain accounts. Test a branch-independent continuity/routing process against current practice at the customer level; keep one assignment across branches and avoid counting transferred revenue as new chain revenue.

Record service delivery, eligible accounts, contacts, costs, branch use, account recognition, and chain-wide contribution. If current branch-loss outreach does not exist, the avoided-contact-cost scenario is inapplicable. Do not sum overlapping branch cohorts or claim war-driven relocation from these observations.

### Test intervention timing

Eligibility begins 28 days after each account’s last recorded chain-wide purchase. Randomize eligible accounts to contact immediately or wait another 28 days and contact only if still inactive. Evaluate both groups for 56 days from randomization, retaining every assigned account in the denominator.

The historical fixed-window cohort is useful evidence; it is not itself an exact “days since last purchase” eligibility calculation. Record the last-purchase date, eligibility date, randomization date, assigned arm, actual contact, any intervening purchase, and follow-up endpoint. An account in the delayed arm that purchases before its planned contact remains assigned to that arm and is not contacted under that rule. Keep accounts with no observed return in the analysis; do not score incomplete follow-up as failure. Maintain customer assignment across branches and handle repeat eligibility without a new inconsistent assignment. Measure contribution, intervention costs, timing of observed return, and service outcomes.

Do not assume 56 days is optimal or that all delayed returns would occur without intervention. Historical late returns might already be influenced by promotions or customer service. A control group and pre-agreed sample size/horizon are needed to estimate incremental value.

## Shared design requirements

Block assignment by calendar period and relevant baseline behavior so arms have comparable Ramadan/Lent and event exposure. Pre-agree a worthwhile dollar improvement, sample size based on variance and clustering, fixed analysis horizon, and service guardrails. Track missing-ID/capture changes and report effects only in the population measured. Evaluate one clear policy per test, rather than choosing a favorable subgroup afterward.
