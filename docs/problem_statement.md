# Accepted problem and current discovery direction

> How can Stories apply changes to maximize profit in times of abrupt change?

The user asks for meaningful business arguments based on pre/post war-context, Ramadan, and Lent comparisons. The final three-part playbook concerns demand/preparation, observational promotion results and uncertainty, and customer continuity with testable intervention timing. The current edits refine the evidence and wording without changing that idea. Garden underperformance remains supporting material.

Use supplied inclusive dates: Ramadan February 17–March 18, Lent February 18–April 2, with March 2 as the war-context cutoff. Compare equal windows, show calendar overlap, and distinguish a disruption-specific change from normal background behavior.

Customer breadth, previously observed accounts, cross-branch continuity, and observed return remain evidence for the third conclusion. The first two conclusions concern selected aggregate trading measures and the observational promotion comparison. Each needs measured evidence, a concrete operating decision, conditional economics, alternative explanations, and a practical test. Do not invent a decline or a causal explanation to strengthen the story.

Customer conclusions apply only to identified accounts. Validate stable IDs across branches; retain missing-ID coverage in every comparison. First appearance is not acquisition, absence from one branch is not chain-wide absence, and absence over 28 days is not permanent churn. The goal is better profit-oriented decisions with data, not guaranteed optimization from incomplete transaction records.
