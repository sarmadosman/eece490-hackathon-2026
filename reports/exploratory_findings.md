# What the useful earlier analyses add

These diagnostics were adapted from the two supplied notebooks and recomputed from the current CSV. Before/after comparisons use February 2–March 1 versus March 2–29 unless stated otherwise. They are descriptive and have different Ramadan/Lent exposure.

## Volume growth can conceal weaker unit contribution

| Harbour category | Units/day change | Gross profit/unit change | Discounted-unit share change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cold Coffee | +16.1% | −5.7% | +24.2 percentage points |
| Desserts | +18.7% | −8.6% | +30.1 percentage points |
| Meals | +41.5% | +4.1% | −7.1 percentage points |
| Bakery | +2.4% | approximately unchanged | +0.1 percentage points |

Source: [category_window_changes.csv](category_window_changes.csv). Cold Coffee and Desserts grew in recorded total gross profit too, but less than their units; increased volume does not establish improved unit economics. Discount exposure and mix are plausible explanations to investigate. These observations do not show that discounts generated incremental demand.

Harbour Meals rose from 65 to 92 recorded units in these windows. That supports a candidate to investigate, with a small base and unknown availability, response, and waste. It does not establish that meal suggestions outperform bakery suggestions.

## Bakery loses relative share while selling more units

Across the chain, Bakery goes from 1,648 to 1,677 units, **+1.8%**, while its share of all units declines by about **1.5 percentage points**. A falling share does not by itself justify cutting preparation. Other categories can simply be growing faster. [Window summary](category_window_summary.csv), [changes](category_window_changes.csv).

## Window lengths strengthen a direction, not a causal claim

Chain-wide Meals units increase **46.2%, 25.6%, and 23.6%** using 14, 28, and 42 days per side. Bakery increases **6.2%, 1.8%, and 10.5%**. The direction is positive across these windows, but the magnitude is sensitive to the window.

Changing the window changes religious-calendar exposure: for example, the 28-day pre-window includes 13 Ramadan days and 12 Lent days, while its post-window includes 17 Ramadan days and 28 Lent days. [Calendar exposure](window_calendar_exposure.csv). These are overlapping checks with calendar confounding, not three independent replications.

## Growth appears around many alternative cutoffs

Across the enumerated alternative 28-day cutoffs, chain-wide order increases range from **5.9% to 10.7%**. The March 2 comparison is **+9.1%**, inside that range. [Alternative cutoffs](alternative_cutoff_comparisons.csv).

This materially qualifies the disruption narrative: a positive March 2 before/after result is not unique and can reflect broad growth. The adjacent-week +12.8% difference remains an observation, but neither that selected week nor these longer windows proves a uniquely abrupt war effect. Read the [calendar analysis](calendar_decision_memo.md) alongside these diagnostics.

## Branch economics require validation before standardizing offers

Across matched products, Garden's median listed-price ratio is **0.953** relative to Harbour and its median recorded-cost ratio is **1.430**. The differences recur across all 45 products, which motivates checking costing and pricing definitions. They do not prove inefficiency or that Harbour's costs are attainable at Garden. [Same-product table](same_product_economics.csv).

The useful operating response is to preserve branch-specific contribution calculations, audit promotions before scaling discounts, compare preparation against actual units rather than share alone, and pilot offer alternatives. The added plots support those decisions; they do not supply customer treatment responses or inventory/waste measurements.
